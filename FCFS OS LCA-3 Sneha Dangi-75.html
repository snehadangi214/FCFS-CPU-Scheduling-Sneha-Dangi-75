Introduction

The First Come, First Served (FCFS) scheduling algorithm is one of the simplest CPU scheduling methods. Processes are served in the order they arrive in the ready queue, without preemption.

In FCFS, once a process starts executing, it runs to completion before the next process begins. This algorithm is fair in terms of arrival time but can lead to long waiting times for shorter processes behind long ones.

How It Works

Processes arrive at different times and enter the ready queue. The CPU picks the first arrived process and runs it until it finishes. Other processes wait in the queue in arrival order. New arrivals cannot interrupt the running process, making FCFS non-preemptive.

The sequence is:

  • Process arrives → enters ready queue
  • Process at front of queue runs in CPU until done
  • Next process moves forward for execution
  • Completed processes leave the system

Calculations

Completion Time (CT): Time at which process finishes.
Turnaround Time (TAT): TAT = CT − AT.
Waiting Time (WT): WT = TAT − BT.

Industrial Application

FCFS scheduling is applied in manufacturing production lines where workstations process jobs/orders in the order they arrive.

In the example shown, three production orders arrive at different times and are processed without interruption in their arrival sequence. This simple approach ensures predictability and respects first-come order.

Advantages and Limitations

Advantages:
  • Simple and easy to implement.
  • Processes execute strictly in arrival order.
  • Fair based on arrival time, no starvation by priority.
Limitations:
  • Long processes delay shorter ones, leading to high waiting times.
  • Non-preemptive nature unsuitable for real-time or interactive tasks.
  • Average waiting time may be high, reducing responsiveness.
